# Gemini Notebook Instructions — HYPERION Six-Slide Presentation

Use all supplied sources as reference material. Create exactly six slides, preserving the reference structure and headings:

1. Title Slide
2. Introduction — A. Current Issue; B. Key Features; C. Core Idea; D. Novelty / Innovation
3. Technical Approach — A. System Architecture; B. Technical Method
4. Feasibility & Viability — A. Feasibility; B. Challenges; C. Risk Mitigation; D. Timeline
5. Impacts & Benefits — A. Expected Benefits; B. Impact vs Effort; C. Social / Mission Relevance
6. Research & References — A. Primary Research; B. Existing Systems / Related Work; C. Differentiation; D. Market / Costs

## Source priority
Use `01-HYPERION-Project-Brief.md` for slide topics and narrative; `02-Architecture-and-Validation.md` for technical wording and evidence labels; `03-Research-Bibliography.md` for citations and limitations; and the original six-slide reference PDF for visual/subtopic structure if included. Treat the original HYPERION specification as authoritative for team-specific requirements only after it is available and reviewed.

## Required accuracy rules
- Keep established research, proposed design, and unvalidated hypotheses explicitly distinct.
- Cite NASA/CSA claims on the relevant slide or in notes using the bibliography links.
- Never imply NASA approval, certification, endorsement, or flight readiness.
- Do not invent results, thresholds, performance, market size, costs, timeline dates, or completed features.
- Describe anomaly flags as anomaly flags, not diagnoses. No autonomous prescribing.
- Label any illustrative or simulated data prominently.
- If the specification conflicts with these sources, flag the discrepancy in notes rather than silently choosing a claim.

## Output style
Make a technical judging presentation with concise slide text, a readable architecture diagram on slide 3, a small feasibility/risk visual on slide 4, and clearly marked intended (not measured) benefits on slide 5. Add speaker notes to every slide with explanation, evidence/caveat, and what is proposed versus demonstrated. Keep citations short on slides and full titles/links in notes or slide 6. Avoid decorative complexity and overly dense paragraphs.

Before finalizing, check that all six slides exist, every reference is traceable, the architecture is labeled proposed, no numeric outcome is unsupported, and the notes do not overclaim.
